# Benchmark study, 2026-10-18 (format and strategy only)

Source: a read-only Instagram study by a research agent on 2026-10-18 (about 30 page loads, one afternoon, IST; no login, follow, like, comment or DM). Every number is "as read then", from public account pages. CLAUDE.md rule 15 applies: this note teaches format and strategy; never copy another account's scripts, wording, visuals, mascot, colours, day list or topic order. No commenter names or comment text were recorded, and none belong here (rule 10). What it means for us: `docs-notes/series-style.md` ("Evidence so far").

## Reel-first or carousel (facts)
- The series reference shows 25 grid posts: 23 carousels (Day 1 to 23, one a day, none missed) and 2 reels. Its Reels tab holds 16 reels, 14 of them not on the grid. A first-time visitor sees a carousel account. Its reels ran about a day ahead of the matching carousel.
- Carousel likes (6 posts older than a day): median about 13.5, 0.2 to 0.9 percent of followers; 0 comments on 22 of 23. Reels (15 older than an hour): median 1,213 views, best 2,612 (0.7x followers). Neither format left the follower base.
- Likes and views are different units. The one day where both formats were read (Day 12) gave the carousel more likes (23) than the reel (12). The study's line that reels earned more reaction rests on one reel (Day 19, 67 likes), so we do not claim it. What we can say: all the large accounts are reel-led, and the small series accounts are not growing on either format.

## What outperformed, and what did not
- Each large account's best reels beat its own median (about 2 to 3x for the ones opened, 3 to 7x for pinned reels). The ones opened were: a career-reality micro-story with a tiny caption (439K against a 227K median), an insider pull quote from a collab (250K against 87K), an "is X dead?" trend question (97.5K against 31.6K). Common thread: winners are rarely "solve problem X" walkthroughs; they sell a stake, a surprise or a topical question. Strongest signal in the data, but only a few reels were opened and the winners are big, face-led accounts.
- Interview or scale scenarios: weak evidence. The one scale-scenario reel read was a low reel by views (14.9K against 31.6K) yet drew 46 comments through its keyword call to action.
- Did not outperform: the daily numbered problem walkthrough alone. The three accounts built on "one problem a day" sit at 0.08 to 0.48x followers. No account in the sample visibly finished a 100 or 150-day numbered DSA series and grew from it (the sample is not exhaustive).

## The Day counter is a label, not a hook
- The series reference puts "Day N/150" on everything. Carousel likes ran 32 on Day 1 (launch), then 6, 10 and 9 on the last three days read. A counter with no hook drew 6 to 32 likes on 3.7K followers. The large accounts grew with no counter. Its best reel (Day 19) is one read and the reason is unknown.
- Read: the counter gives identity and a reason to follow; it gives no reason to stop scrolling. No evidence either way that it harms. Its covers lead with a giant day number; ours lead with the headline (rule 6).

## Comment-keyword call to action
- Two accounts ask viewers to comment one keyword to get the solution or resource. Reels read: 91 comments (97.5K views), 46 comments (a weak 14.9K reel), a handful (6.7K views). The account with a link-in-bio ask only had 0 comments on 22 of 23 carousels. So a keyword raises the comment count; the study does not show it raises reach.
- It needs an automation that answers each comment or DM. That is the owner's own, owner-approved tool (rule 10). Agents never post, reply, like, follow or DM (rule 9): they may propose the keyword and draft the delivery text, nothing more.

## Posting times (observed, not proven causal)
- Most reels in the sample went up between about 17:15 and 23:15 IST: 18:45 to 20:50, 18:30 to 20:30, 18:35, 20:30 to 23:15, 17:15 to 20:00. The series reference posts reels near 18:00 and carousels 09:19 to 16:31.
- The series reference (the smallest active account) posts reels near 18:00, the same hour as the strong ones, so the clock does not explain the gap. Treat an evening IST window as something to test on our own numbers.

## Hashtags
Carousels of the series reference: 17 to 21 tags. Its reels: 5. Large accounts: 0 to 6 (one uses none, another 5 to 6); a small daily-problem account uses 3. Our rule stays at three (ig-caption).

## Avoid list (format)
1. Raw markdown asterisks in a caption (one reel caption showed them).
2. 17 to 21 hashtags on every post.
3. Dense slides with small type; unreadable on a phone.
4. Five of 13 slides spent on the same code in five languages.
5. A link in bio as the only call to action.
6. Reels hidden off the grid, so the profile reads as a carousel account.
7. Covers that lead with the Day number instead of the viewer's stake.
8. A series identity with no hook.
9. Copying another account's colours, mascot or layout.

## Limits of the data
- The reel videos did not render: no hook was heard, no reel length seen, no spoken language checked. Hook evidence is from captions and on-screen text only. Length targets are our design choice, not an observation.
- Likes and comments were read on 1 or 2 reels per creator; the rest is view counts. About 8 creators, carousels of others not browsed, so gaps named below are hypotheses.
- Hinglish against English is confounded: the largest accounts are face-led career brands, and no pure Hinglish DSA series was found.
- Some runs are old (Oct 2024, 2023). How the series reference gained its followers is unknown. Statements from the study's own knowledge, not a fetched page, are unverified.

## Gaps nobody in the sample served (hypotheses, not facts)
A faceless, voiceover-plus-animation, one-idea-a-day Hinglish reel series whose reels show on the grid; pattern-first teaching instead of one isolated problem each day; interview stakes (follow-ups, where candidates fail) without a face.
