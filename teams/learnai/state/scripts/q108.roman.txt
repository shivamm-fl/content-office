A: France ki capital poochhi… Bot ne teen lines mein jawab diya! || jab Paris ek word mein bataya jaa sakta hai, to itna extra effort kyun? || Interview mein poochhen: Does chain-of-thought prompting always improve performance?
B: tumhara answer hona chahiye: No, not always. || Chain-of-thought prompting ka matlab hai, model se problem ko step by step solve karne ko kehna. || ye complex, multi-step problems mein help kar sakta hai. || lekin har simple question ke liye ye zaruri nahi. || chalo, teen situations dekhte hain.

E: ab important twist! || agar tum reasoning-focused model use kar rahe ho, to zaruri nahi ki har baar usko think step by step bolo. || wo pehle se hi multi-step reasoning ke liye designed ho sakta hai. || Extra instruction kabhi redundant ho sakti hai, aur performance bhi model ke hisab se change ho sakti hai.
F: Interview mein confidently bolo: || Chain-of-thought prompting can improve performance on complex, multi-step tasks, but it isn't universally beneficial. || For simple tasks, it may increase output tokens, latency, and cost without adding value. || I'd evaluate both approaches on representative examples and compare accuracy, latency, and cost. || For reasoning models, I'd also test whether explicit step-by-step instructions provide any additional benefit.
