Repetition penalty: why a chatbot repeats itself

Day 20/150: greedy decoding always picks the single most likely next word, so a language model can fall into a loop and repeat the same text. A repetition penalty lowers the score of words already used, so a fresh word can win. A value of 1.0 means no penalty; the CTRL paper's authors suggest around 1.2. A penalty that is too strong, or no_repeat_ngram_size, can also hurt words that must repeat, such as the city name New York. The scores in the reel are invented toy numbers and the subtraction rule is a toy, not the library's formula.
