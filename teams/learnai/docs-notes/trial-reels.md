# Trial reels: try a reel out on non-followers first (owner decision 2026-10-20)

A trial reel is an Instagram reel shown to non-followers first. We use it to find out whether something new works before it goes to our followers and onto the grid: a new hook, a new format, a new kind of idea, a different length. **One reel is enough**: a single trial reel is the normal case. Two or three versions of the same reel are an occasional extra, when comparing is the point. The strategist and the growth analyst suggest what is worth trying, the Producer keeps it on the board, the owner decides and posts (company rule 1: agents never post).

## What a trial reel is (verified, pages fetched 2026-10-20)
- A reel shown to non-followers first. Followers do not see it in Feed or the Reels tab; some may still meet it in a DM share or on a page of reels with the same audio, location or filter. It stays off the profile grid and Reels tab unless it is later shared with everyone. https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels (post of 10 Dec 2024), https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first/
- How: make the reel as usual, turn on the "Trial" toggle before sharing. Same sources.
- Graduating to followers: manual ("share with everyone", the reel then goes to the grid and to followers on all surfaces), or automatic: chosen when the trial is made, Instagram shares it with followers "if it determines the reel is performing well based on views in the first 72 hours"; the setting can be changed at any time. What "performing well" means in numbers is not published. Same sources.
- Insights: about 24 hours after sharing the reels viewer shows views, likes, comments and shares, and Instagram compares the trial with the account's earlier trials. Same sources. Whether watch time, skip rate and follows are shown for a trial is NOT stated; check in the app before relying on them.
- Account: "You must have a professional account to use trial reels"; some creators without one may have access in testing. https://creators.instagram.com/reels. The eligibility details live in the Help Center, which we could not fetch: unverified. (Third-party pages claim a minimum follower count; that is not from Meta and we do not use it.) Our account already posts through the API, which is for professional accounts, so we expect to be eligible.
- Meta does not publish how many non-followers see a trial or how they are chosen, and no cap on the number of trials.
- API: the content publishing guide has a "Trial Reels posts" section. "To publish a trial reel, create a container for the video and include a valid `trial_params` parameter", with `graduation_strategy` `MANUAL` (graduated by hand in the app) or `SS_PERFORMANCE` (graduated automatically if the trial performs well); example: POST `/media` with `media_type` `REELS`, `video_url` and `"trial_params":{"graduation_strategy":"MANUAL"}`. https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/ . Unverified: whether `share_to_feed` and `cover_url` may be sent together with `trial_params`, and whether the insights API returns trial numbers.

## Posting situation today
The office cannot post a trial reel. The Calendar's Post sheet offers Post now and Put on a slot only; the hand-over workflow and the poster (`ig_post.py`) create the reel container without `trial_params` and then cross-post to Facebook and YouTube, which are public. So today the owner posts a trial reel by hand: take the video and the caption from the reel's final card, share it in the Instagram app with the Trial toggle on, choose manual graduation, and do not tap Post for that item in the Calendar afterwards (that would make it a normal public post). Tell the Producer the date and time it went out so the experiment line gets it.
What would make it one tap: a trial switch in the Post sheet (Post now only), `trial` in the publish request and the hand-over inputs, `trial_params` in the poster, and no Facebook or YouTube cross-post for a trial. Not built yet; the owner decides.

## How we use it
- Any reel can go out as a trial: a new hook, a new format, a new kind of idea, a different length, a topic we have no numbers for. The strategist, the growth analyst and the Producer suggest which of the coming reels is worth it; the owner decides.
- A trial reel is not on the profile grid until it is shared with everyone. A numbered series (the interview reels, question 001, 002) keeps its number on the grid: when the grid should have a number, post one version normally and try the other as a trial (T01: q105 normal, q107 trial).
- Versions: now and then, when comparing is the point. Each version is a normal reel with its own office id, the next free number of its track (a second version of q105 is q107), and `"trial": {"tries": "<what is new>", "of": "<id of the first version>"}` in its queue entry; a single trial reel needs only `"tries"`. The cover count of a version is the number of the question it repeats. Say honestly when more than one thing differs between versions.
- Before it goes out, one line in `state/experiments.md`: what is new and what we will look at.

## Reading a trial
- 72 hours after it went out (the window Instagram itself uses for automatic graduation). Look at: reach to non-followers, the 3-second hold (1 minus the skip rate), the average watch time as a share of the reel's length, shares, saves and follows per 1,000 reach. Say which of these the app or `state/insights.json` really shows for a trial; never estimate a missing one.
- Compare with our usual numbers (`state/learnings.md`: reels posted 2026-10-15 or later, n=18, median about 1,500 views, 59 % skip, 12 s average watch). A few hundred views prove nothing: say promising or weak, not "winner". Between two versions, a gap of a few points on a few hundred views is noise.
- The result goes into `state/experiments.md` (what we tried, the numbers, what it suggests) and, if it teaches something, a short rule into `state/team_playbook.md`. When a trial did well, the Producer tells the owner to share it with everyone (it then lands on the grid). A rule that should bind the whole team goes to the owner as a `learning` card, as always.
